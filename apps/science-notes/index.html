IV.Answer the following

1What is the skeleton made of?

The skeleton is made of bones. It is a strong frame that supports our body from inside.

2How does the skeleton protect the brain?

The skull protects the brain. It also gives our face its shape.

3Which organs does the ribcage protect?

The ribcage protects the organs in the chest, such as the heart and lungs, from damage.

4Write the functions of the skeleton.

Our skeleton has four main functions:
  1. It protects the main organs of our body.
  2. It makes our body firm and gives it its shape.
  3. It allows us to move, walk and run.
  4. It grows as we grow.

5Why do we need medicines?

We take medicines to make us better when we are ill, and to stop us from getting ill.

6What is the difference between vertebrates and invertebrates?

  • Vertebrates: animals with a skeleton (backbone) inside their bodies. Example: lion, tiger.
  • Invertebrates: animals without a skeleton inside their bodies. Example: earthworm, jellyfish.

7Draw pictures of a vertebrate and an invertebrate.

Vertebrate: a fish (it has bones). Invertebrate: a worm.

IV.Short answers

1If energy is not used up or destroyed, where does it go?

The energy changes form, or it moves into the surrounding environment.

2What are herbivores?

Animals that eat only plants are called herbivores. Example: deer, zebra.

3What are carnivores?

Animals that eat the flesh of other animals are called carnivores. Example: lion, tiger.

4What are omnivores?

Animals that eat both plants and animals are called omnivores. Example: dog, bear.

5Why are plants called producers?

Plants are called producers because they make (produce) their own food.

6Why are animals called consumers?

Animals are called consumers because they eat (consume) plants or other animals.

The example was missing. Example: a cup of hot tea cools down. Its heat energy has not disappeared; it has moved into the air around it.

2Why do our bodies need energy?

Our body needs energy to carry out activities such as running, playing and growing.

3Where do living things get their energy from?

Living things get their energy from food. Plants use energy from the Sun to make their food.

4Why do all living things need energy?

All living things need energy to move, grow, reproduce and carry out other activities. Plants and animals will not be healthy if they do not have enough energy.

I.Answer the following

1What do you mean by producers and consumers?

  • Producers: living things that make their own food. Example: plants.
  • Consumers: living things that eat (consume) the food made by plants, or eat other animals. Example: rabbit.

2What are herbivores, carnivores and omnivores?

  • Herbivores: animals that eat only plants. Example: rabbit, deer.
  • Carnivores: animals that eat the flesh of other animals. Example: tiger, lion.
  • Omnivores: animals that eat both plants and animals. Example: dog, bear.

3What do you mean by predator and prey?

  • Predator: an animal that hunts, kills and eats other animals. Example: lion.
  • Prey: an animal that is hunted and eaten by a predator. Example: deer.

The notebook got "predator" backwards; it described prey. Learn the answer above.

4What do you mean by vertebrates, invertebrates and exoskeleton?

  • Vertebrates: animals with a skeleton inside their bodies. Example: lion, tiger.
  • Invertebrates: animals without a skeleton inside their bodies. Example: earthworm, jellyfish.
  • Exoskeleton: some animals have a hard skeleton on the outside of their body. Example: beetle, bee.

5Draw the particle arrangement of solids, liquids and gases.

6Write the properties of solids, liquids and gases.

  • Solid: particles are tightly packed together. A solid keeps its own shape; it does not take the shape of its container.
  • Liquid: particles are loosely packed and can move around. A liquid changes shape and takes the shape of its container.
  • Gas: particles are far apart and move freely in all directions. A gas spreads out to fill the whole container.
